# A Metaphor: The Restaurant

**Principle: every piece of work has a *what* and a *how*. First decide the right thing to do, then do the thing right. A restaurant preparing for an event shows both, and every step of this book.**

Picture a restaurant asked to cater an event. Follow what happens, first the *what*, then the *how*.

Not every step is needed every time. It depends on how complex the situation is. If you are planning a simple dinner at home, you will skip most of them. A large event needs all of them.



## Do the right thing: wear the product manager's hat

The question is: **what should we cook?** The job is to design the menu for the event.

**[Collect](#a2a).** Ask people for data and details: the client, the manager, the owner. Gather what you need to know about what to cook: how many people are coming, their preferences, the occasion, the weather, what competitors offer.

**[Process](#a2b).** Go through everything you collected, keeping the goal in mind. Check that the details are current and confirmed. Is the head count final? Slice and dice the details as the goal requires. A different goal means processing the same details in a different way.

**[Organize](#a2c).** Group and label what you have collected. Keep the client's preferences together. Keep the event details up front, because you need them now and only for this event. Add new suppliers to the permanent supplier list, in the background, ready for next time.

**[Interpret](#a2d).** Put on the sense-making hat. Go through the information to understand the client, the event and the culture around it. Read between the lines. Digest it all, then decide what the menu should be.

**[Structure](#a2e).** How the menu is laid out depends on who will read it. The restaurant owner needs it framed around sales and competition. The client needs it framed around budget and satisfaction. The guests need only the courses.



## Do the thing right: wear the project manager's hat

The job now is to **get the resources needed and bring the menu to the table.**

**[Use of data and information](#b1).** The menu is information, and nobody eats a menu. The kitchen turns it into what it must do: recipes, a shopping list, and a prep list for each cook. The data collected earlier keeps working here. The head count sets the quantities, and the client's preferences decide what goes on each plate.

**[Plan and prepare](#b2).** Confirm the menu, the scope and the items, and clarify anything still open. Check which resources are available, estimate the timeline, prepare the plan and get ready.

**[Execute and monitor](#b3).** Put the plan in motion and cook. Watch the progress throughout.

**[Review and reflect](#b4).** Check the results against the goals, gather feedback and capture the lessons learned. Share the lessons and discuss them with the people who chose the menu.

**[Adapt and iterate](#b5).** The cycle starts again whenever the information changes or a new need or event comes along.



## What to do differently

Pick a task you have coming up. Before you start, ask which hat it needs first. If you can't yet say *what* you are making and for whom, you are still wearing the product manager's hat, however keen you are to start cooking.
