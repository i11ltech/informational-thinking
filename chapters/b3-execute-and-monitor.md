# Execute and Monitor

**Principle: once the plan is ready, act. Then watch the work while it happens, not only at the end. Judge progress by what is finished, not by how busy things look, and look hardest at work that has stopped moving.**



## Act

The plan exists so that you can start. Waiting for complete confidence is a way of not starting. Project management advice on turning knowledge into practice puts it simply: take small, imperfect action instead of waiting for endless planning. You will learn more from the first hour of doing than from another day of preparing.

In [the restaurant](#metaphor), this is the moment the kitchen starts cooking. The menu has been confirmed and the plan is made. From here, the information that matters most comes from the work itself.



## Watch while it happens

A cook watches the dishes while they cook, not just when they reach the table. By then, it is too late to fix anything.

Monitoring means collecting data about the work while it is under way, and it follows the same rules as [Collect](#a2a): know what you are looking for before you look. You decided most of it already. [Project Management Thinking](#b0) gave you an early warning to watch for. [Plan and Prepare](#b2) gave you a measure of success, a guardrail and a buffer. Check them at the times you set, not only when something feels wrong.



## Count what is finished

"How far along are you?" usually gets a percentage, and percentages are guesses. Project managers prefer to count **finished** work: how many items have actually been completed this week. Busy is not the same as done.

Watch the scope as well as the speed. If you track finished work and the total amount of work separately, you can tell two problems apart that look the same from the inside: work going slowly, and work quietly growing. They need different fixes.



## Look for what has stopped moving

The earliest warning of trouble is usually not a missed deadline. It is a task that has sat untouched for longer than tasks like it normally take. Something is blocking it, and nobody has said so yet.

A related warning is work piling up at one stage, such as many things waiting for review. That is where the [bottleneck](#b0) is. Adding more work upstream will only make the pile bigger.

*Kanban*, one of the Agile methods, is built around both ideas. It makes the work visible on a board, with columns for each stage, and limits how many things can be in progress at once, so that a pile-up shows straight away.



## The bridge: delivery data

Project management has a whole toolkit for monitoring delivery: boards, flow charts, burn-up charts and schedule indices. They exist to answer the same few questions: What is finished? What is stuck? Is the scope growing? Are we still on course?

**Where this book departs:** those tools are built for teams with weeks of work and many tasks in flight. Your work is often a handful of steps over hours, some of them done by an AI tool. A three-column list (to do, doing, done) is enough of a board. The bigger change is what you watch. AI tools produce results fast, and each result often becomes the input to the next step. So monitoring means checking each intermediate result *before* you build on it, rather than reviewing the whole thing at the end. An error caught after one step costs one step. Caught at the end, it can cost all of them.

Monitoring is not the same as fixing. When a check shows a problem, note it. Stop only if a guardrail has tripped. Deciding how to change course belongs to [Review and Reflect](#b4) and [Adapt and Iterate](#b5).



## What to do differently

While you carry out your plan from [Plan and Prepare](#b2), keep a three-column list: **to do, doing, done**. At each check time you set, ask:

1. **What has finished** since the last check?
2. **What hasn't moved?** Anything in "doing" for longer than usual is your first warning.
3. **Has the scope grown?** Has anything new appeared in "to do" that wasn't in your plan?
4. **Is the early warning showing?** Has a guardrail tripped?

If you're working with an AI tool, add one more: **did I check this result before using it in the next step?**
