# The 5P's of Prompting

**Principle: the 5P's are an easy checklist to remember for a prompt: Prime, Persona, Privacy, Product, Polish. Use them as a check, but start one step earlier. Before you prime the model, know the decision the answer is for.**



## The five P's

The 5P's method was written for professionals, originally for mediators and lawyers, who use AI in their work:

* **Prime: start with context.** Set the scene: the facts, the time frame and the jurisdiction or domain. The more relevant background you give, the more the answer fits your case.
* **Persona: set the tone and expertise.** Say what role the model should take, and how formal or expert the answer should be. An expert legal analysis and a plain-language explanation of the same question are very different answers.
* **Privacy: protect sensitive information.** Treat every prompt as if it could be shared. When in doubt, anonymise names and details, or use placeholders.
* **Product: define the goal.** Say what you want delivered: a 200-word summary, a list of the five biggest differences, an email to a client.
* **Polish: refine.** The first answer isn't the last. Follow up to fill gaps, shift the focus, adjust the tone, or ask for the weaknesses in what was proposed.

One version of the method swaps Privacy for **Prompt**: writing a clear, specific sequence of instructions. Both are worth keeping in mind.



## How the 5P's map onto a strong prompt

Set against [The Anatomy of a Strong Prompt](#f2):

| 5P's | Anatomy |
|---|---|
| Prime | Context |
| Persona | Audience and constraints (tone, level) |
| Product | Task and format |
| Polish | Checking against success criteria, then iterating |
| Privacy | What to leave out of the context |

Privacy is the P that other checklists tend to miss. It is also an informational choice: deciding what the model doesn't need to know is as much a part of the prompt as deciding what it does. [Context Without Clutter](#i1) returns to this.



## What the 5P's leave out

Two things from informational thinking are missing from the list:

* **The decision.** Prime starts with context, but context for what? [From Topic to Task](#f4) covers the step before Prime: knowing what you will do with the answer.
* **Success criteria.** Polish says to refine until the answer meets your needs, but not how you'll know it does. Decide that before you send the prompt, not after you read the reply. Otherwise any plausible answer will seem good enough.



## The bridge

The 5P's are a practitioner's checklist, built for people who need results from AI in a professional setting. Informational thinking adds the *why* behind each P. Prime is [collecting](book.html#a2a) the right context. Privacy is cutting what doesn't belong, as in [Process](book.html#a2b). Product is [structuring](book.html#a2e) for the reader. Polish is [Review and Reflect](book.html#b4) and [Adapt and Iterate](book.html#b5), done in minutes.

**Where this departs:** the 5P's treat the prompt as the start of the work. Informational thinking treats it as the middle. The thinking about what you need happens before the first P, and the judgment about whether you got it happens after the last.



## What to do differently

Write your next prompt with the 5P's, and add two lines around them:

* **Before Prime:** "I need this to decide …"
* **After Polish:** "This answer is good enough when …"

If you can't write the first line, you're not ready to prompt. If you can't write the second, you won't know when to stop polishing.
